# Ticket 30 — second decision round accepted

The human accepted all five recommendations with “Agree.” Round 1 is also accepted. These are operating thresholds and verification requirements, not current venue measurements, prices, permissions or authorization to spend.

1. Readiness and health. Before injection require five continuous minutes of healthy baseline with all Faults at their declared baseline values, stable intended workload replicas, no unexpected restarts or critical infrastructure Alerts, working mandatory service boundaries and fresh system telemetry. Required health observations must be no older than 60 seconds. Require LGTM and load-generator working sets below 80 percent of their verified configured limits, at least 25 percent node memory available and no node pressure/evictions; these are admission guardrails, not duration guarantees. Verify actual selected metric semantics, limits and freshness before relying on the checks. During a Fault, do not classify its expected symptoms/restarts as unrelated venue failure; node pressure, exhausted mandatory backends and unexpected critical infrastructure conditions still hold admission. Missing/stale required health evidence fails readiness. Also apply existing OPS eligibility, recovery/Change reconciliation, budget, capture and mediated-client gates. The five-minute baseline must finish before the accepted age-30 start cutoff; do not reset cluster age to make it fit.
2. Session admission timing. The planned live window lasts 30 minutes from the recorded session start. Beyond that window, mark the live lifecycle overrun and switch audience presentation to labelled replay; allow already-admitted Incident/recovery work within remaining venue and spend bounds without pretending it completed on stage. Permit no model Run launch at or after cluster age 85 minutes, so the existing five-minute maximum fits inside age 90; earlier health or budget holds still win. At 90 minutes all ordinary admissions remain held and the operator performs recovery/handoff/teardown. No replacement injection, model fallback, new cluster or automatic budget extension is authorized by a missed gate. Report actual stage and recovery durations separately.
3. Cloud budget. Establish a separate $10 Monday–Sunday America/New_York venue-spend planning envelope, reserving $2 before each cluster creation attempt, including failed setups and replacements. Count all attributable node, control-plane, storage, network and residual-resource charges; verify applicable current prices and billing behavior before creation. A $2 reservation is not a guaranteed maximum charge. Admit only when known spend plus outstanding reservations/exposure leaves defensible room; unknown charges remain unresolved and late bills are reconciled without double-counting. No automatic top-up, recreation or higher resource tier. This budget is separate from ADR 0013's $150 model envelope and does not itself authorize provisioning or purchases. The existing weekly journal/accounting specification must preserve cloud totals across cluster destruction, not keep the only budget record inside that cluster.
4. Protected cleanup and verification. The operator owns teardown. Before destruction, stop ordinary admissions, reconcile/cancel active work under existing deadlines, undo or explicitly hand off Fault state, and save a private off-cluster recovery manifest plus required artifacts. Include environment identity, outstanding Notification/Run/Change IDs and stages, uncertain external effects and evidence references, OPS disposition, weekly model/cloud spend reservations, private audit artifacts/digests and the named operator accepting any unresolved obligations. Exclude credentials and do not relabel an unresolved obligation as resolved. Verify the exported copy by read-back and digest comparison before destroying its only source. Preserve external OPS/Confluence history and account billing. Confirm provider resource inventory after deletion, including residual volumes/IPs and separately billable resources; command exit alone is not cleanup proof. Record uncertain deletion or remaining cost and escalate to the operator, never silently declare teardown complete. Emergency restoration keeps priority; failed export means an explicit exceptional human decision about potential evidence loss, not an automatic deletion path.
5. Acceptance and operator runbook. Graduate a bounded implementation-ready lifecycle specification covering state transitions, age source, clock/restart behavior, health queries, resource scope, budgets, export and verified cleanup. Offline acceptance must exercise the actual admission/clock/health/accounting/handoff boundaries, including stale data, age cutoffs, missing limits, expected Fault symptoms versus unrelated failures, concurrent reservations, interrupted export and uncertain deletion. Separate future intended-venue acceptance: one no-Fault 90-minute baseline observation plus the three already budgeted candidate qualification lifecycles on fresh venues, with truthful actual runtime/health/cost and cleanup evidence. Allocate the baseline venue to the cloud envelope; it requires no model Runs. This is a bounded operational gate, not a leak diagnosis or a proof of universal 90-minute safety. Failures retain replay and graduate specific tuning or measurement work rather than silently increasing limits. No baseline, qualification or provisioning is run by accepting this plan.

After acceptance: record the ADR and lifecycle specification ticket; align tickets 32/37/38/39/41 and the earlier venue ADR without changing runtime, rule or Skill code. Unknown spending/effects and active recovery survive timer boundaries and teardown handoff.
