### Standing preferences, decided while charting on 2026-09-15

- Date: soft target 2026-10-31. Slot: assume thirty minutes and one lifecycle, so that one Run may think for five minutes on screen.
- Live demo and public repo both. Public from the first push.
- Plan, don't do. The two specs are the handoff.
- Report-only. The Run never remediates; remediation is a suggestion in the Report.
- One Incident per Fault; the Match is a judgment (ADR 0006).
- Ground truth rule: every Fault has a documented Ground truth, and a Report is judged by whether its Suggested root cause names its **Mechanism** — naming the Trigger is not a diagnosis (ADR 0008). A ticket that adds a Fault writes its Ground truth.
- Citation rule: every claim in a Report's root-cause section cites evidence the Run retrieved.
- Eyes and Hands both go through a sentinel. Anonymous Grafana access goes away.
- The Kubernetes signal class is **pod status retrieved through read-only `kubectl`**, not Kubernetes Events. Three nominations died in a row: ticket 05's restart loop, ticket 01's Event, and the OOMKill premise ticket 10 chose on — **`OOMKilled` emits no Kubernetes Event**, it is a `containerStatuses[].lastState.terminated.reason` carried by neither Loki nor Prometheus. The Fault that carries the class is `emailMemoryLeak`, and this makes [Eyes](issues/12-eyes.md) a hard dependency rather than a parallel concern. **Measured and confirmed** by [Verify the signal surface](issues/27-verify-the-signal-surface-and-settle-the-fault-gates.md): a real OOMKill produced `lastState.terminated` = `OOMKilled`/`exitCode 137` and **eight Kubernetes Events, all `Normal`, none naming OOM**. The Float gate that could have killed `emailMemoryLeak` **does not fire**, so there is no fourth refutation. **Corrected again by [Alert rules for a Cascade](issues/28-alert-rules-for-a-cascade.md):** the class *does* produce a usable `Warning` Kubernetes Event, just not the one anyone looked for — **`BackOff`**, "Back-off restarting failed container email", reaches Loki from **t+219 s** with `deprecatedCount` rising to 5. Ticket 27's "eight Events, all `Normal`" was measured after **one** restart, before any back-off could exist. `OOMKilled` still emits no Event. It earns no rule of its own — it is the same fact as the restart count — and belongs in the Skill's query guide as corroborating evidence.
- A Fault is **exactly one flag**; compound Faults are out of scope. A Ground truth is two layers, Mechanism and Trigger, scored on the Mechanism alone, and it never enters the Run's world (ADR 0008). The traffic level is venue, not Fault: `make up` pins `loadGeneratorVUs` once. One presenter action means one command, not one API call — every Fault's undo restarts a service.
- Cascade breadth comes from **instantiation, not rule count**: two to four alertable conditions with `by (service_name)` give six to nine Alerts, each with its own Fingerprint. Writing ten rules for one Fault would manufacture the alert fatigue the demo argues against. **Settled by [Alert rules for a Cascade](issues/28-alert-rules-for-a-cascade.md):** the rules describe the **system, not the Faults** — one standing set of six in folder `demo`, so any Fault lights up whichever services it touches and the fallback needs no rule set of its own. Severity is graded `critical`/`warning` and **never any other value**, which the Skill maps silently to Sev-3. **Cascade sizes confirmed by [Measure the Cascade against real rules](issues/29-measure-the-cascade-against-real-rules.md): 7, 3 and 1 exactly**, with `paymentUnreachable`'s composition right service by service. But **the grouping claim is refuted**: `group_by: [grafana_folder, alertname]` collapses instances of one rule only when they are *simultaneous*, and C1's members joined over 120 s, so the 7 Alerts arrived as **four Notifications and four Runs, not two** — and `emailMemoryLeak`'s three Alerts are three alertnames and cannot collapse at all. **Grouping never collapses across rules**, so "many alerts, one incident" is the Run's Match to produce, or the global policy's by dropping `alertname`.
- Cloud is allowed: if the laptop cannot hold the simulation, or the demo wants sharing and accessibility, deploy to cloud. DigitalOcean is the one cloud with tooling on this machine.
- Recognizable system preferred: the OpenTelemetry Demo if it fits; a hand-rolled topology copying its shape if not.
- Many-to-one: [ticket 14](issues/14-many-to-one-under-a-cascade.md#answer) keeps folder/alertname grouping with 10s group timers and 10m repeats. The Receiver suppresses value-aware exact repeats and coalesces pending Alerts while running one Run at a time. The Run judges eligible open Incidents, so Reports grow through appended evidence and explicit corrections; a Run may handle several Notifications.
- Memory is three stores: Incidents in OPS, pages in a Confluence space reached through `confluence-as` as the peer of `jira-as`, and the Memory directory. [Memory](issues/13-memory.md#answer) assigns OPS authoritative Incident state, Confluence reviewed references, and the directory cited learning within a rehearsal (ADR 0009).
- Model: fit-to-slot. Opus 5 at high effort was the hypothesis; it was tested and does not fit five minutes, see [Does a high-effort Run fit the slot](issues/11-does-a-high-effort-run-fit-the-slot.md).
- Structured events, the fourth signal: Kubernetes Events, Changes, and Run events from the Transcript and the harness's own telemetry export.
- Carried from chapter one: one command brings everything up; one presenter action starts the story; a replay script survives a broken Grafana.

What moved: **two of six absence services do not go absent** (shipping and cart keep serving pre-charge traffic, so a service-level rule never fires), payment is 0.0042/s not "exactly zero", the SERVER-only checkout ratio is **1.0000** not ~18%, **Fault 2 raises only 2–3 Alerts**, and Fault 3's diagnostic path is written against a **line filter that cannot work**. First-Alert-to-last-Alert needs rules and became [Measure the Cascade against real rules](issues/29-measure-the-cascade-against-real-rules.md).

- [Alert rules for a Cascade](issues/28-alert-rules-for-a-cascade.md) — **six rules that describe the system, not the Faults**, loadable on branch `rules/cascade`. Every rule as first drafted was refuted. The error condition is an **absolute rate `> 0.1/s`, not a ratio**: the ratio **inverts** on frontend-proxy — `0.00705` under the Fault against `0.00808` with every flag off — and returns `NaN` on the three services the absence rule already owns, yielding 1 Alert where the rate yields 3. The email memory rule latches on `max_over_time([5m])` because the naive `> 70Mi` form goes Firing→Resolved **five times in 1181 s**, which under ADR 0004 is **five Incidents from one Fault**; `keepFiringFor` would say it directly and is absent from Grafana 12.0.1's file-provisioning struct, so the constraint picked the better condition. Three rules name a service and both reasons are measured: a generic memory ratio is refuted because **checkout sits at 90% of its limit at steady state**, and a generic log rule is impossible because `severity_text`/`detected_level` are structured metadata, not stream labels. New invariant: **never `or vector(0)` on a `by()` rule** — `vector(0)` returns an *unlabelled* series, so the Resolved can never Match the Incident. Label keys frozen at `{severity, service, cascade}`; adding one later orphans every open Incident silently. **Nothing here has ever been evaluated by Grafana at the venue**, and the biggest correction is to the verification layer itself: two verifiers killed both of the live Fault's conditions on a `[1m]` artifact misread as a `[5m]` result.

- [Measure the Cascade against real rules](issues/29-measure-the-cascade-against-real-rules.md) — **the rules work and the fire edge is t+241 s, not the projected 7.5 min**, for $0.38 and 2 h 39 m. Ticket 28's Cascade *sizes* are exact — 7, 3, 1 — and `paymentUnreachable`'s composition is right service by service; Grafana lagged every condition by exactly its `for` plus one eval across seven firings, with no divergence between channels anywhere. What must change is **C1's `> 0.1/s`, and on the fault side, not the baseline side**: 30 flags-off samples clear it by 8×, but checkout and frontend-proxy run a mean of 1.10× it and read **exactly 0.1000** twice mid-Fault, so both resolved while the Fault ran and re-fired — **7 Alerts became 9 Incidents**. A replay of every sample gives a clean band 0.02–0.05; **0.03/s** is its centre. `adFailure` confirms it from the other side: at 0.1/s the designated fallback raises **no Cascade at all**, and its one Alert fired 144 s *after* the undo, caused by the remediation. Also: **grouping never collapses across rules** (7 Alerts → 4 Notifications → 4 Runs), **`KeepLast` would strand both Loki rules** because Loki returns an empty vector not 0, the **Fingerprint is per (rule, label-set) not per Incident**, and **C4 is cluster-wide** — it fired on a `load-generator` OOMKill at 116 min that nobody injected.
